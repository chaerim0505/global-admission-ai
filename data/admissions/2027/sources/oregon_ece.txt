Advance your research network with a master's or Ph.D. in electrical and computer engineering
As a master’s or Ph.D. student in electrical and computer engineering, you will work with top experts in fields like energy systems, integrated electronics, signal processing, networking, and materials science, who are driving innovations for health solutions, renewable energy, semiconductors, and many other applications.
Our collaborative environment opens up opportunities for projects in areas such as agriculture, artificial intelligence, forestry, robotics, oceanography, and beyond. Our strong connection to numerous companies like Analog Devices, Intel, Garmin, HP, Tektronix, and Micron, motivates applied research and gives students opportunities to get hands-on experiences through research and internships.

Admissions Process for M.S. and Ph.D.
Admissions Process for M.S. and Ph.D.
Confirm You Meet the Minimum Academic Requirements.
Every student must meet a set of academic requirements to be admitted to any graduate program at Oregon State.
Domestic applicants: Confirm you meet the
minimum academic requirements
for graduate school at Oregon State.
International applicants must meet the
minimum requirements for international applicants
.
Apply Online.
Prospective graduate students may access the OSU Graduate Programs application directly through
the Office of Graduate Education
. The application will walk you through submitting all the required materials and paying your fee.
Gather Your Transcripts.
Provide transcripts from all institutions where coursework was completed or attempted in pursuit of an undergraduate or graduate degree.
This includes, for example, coursework completed at a community college and later transferred to a four-year institution and counted toward a bachelor’s degree.
During the application process, we accept unofficial or official transcripts.
If you are admitted to OSU and you applied using unofficial transcripts, you will need to
send official transcripts
to the Office of Graduate Education. When ready to send official transcripts, please send electronic transcripts from the U.S. institutions you attended. When ordering a transcript as part of your graduate admissions record, select “Oregon State University-Graduate Admissions” as the recipient.
Request Three Letters of Recommendation.
A letter of recommendation is a written document from a professor, supervisor, or other non-family individual who can speak to your academic or research successes/potential, character and/or leadership qualities, overall progress, and/or other achievements.
As part of the application for admission, you will provide the names and email addresses of three people to write you a letter of recommendation (or “letter of reference”). The letter of reference system triggers an email to each reference writer and enables them to submit a confidential electronic letter for you.
Ask the people you’d like to be your letter writers before you list them on an application. Explain what program you are applying for and why you are interested in pursuing graduate school. This will help them gauge what to highlight in the letter. Clarify the deadline and let them know to expect an email with submission instructions. The OSU Office of Graduate Education has additional
helpful suggestions
.
Write a Statement of Objectives.
In a one-page statement, outline your motivations for pursuing graduate study, your academic and professional goals, and why our specific program is a good fit for you.
Identify Faculty of Interest (for Ph.D. applicants).
All incoming Ph.D. students will be matched with a faculty advisor when admitted into the program.
The application allows you to list faculty with whom you are most interested in working if you were to be accepted into the program. Explore our
research areas
and our
individual faculty pages
to identify faculty whose work interests you.
You do not need to secure a faculty advisor before applying to the program. Faculty who are recruiting students will review applications in our applicant pool. While not required, you can feel free to reach out to potential faculty of interest directly, though some prefer to wait until applications are received to consider your materials/interest.
Prepare Your CV/Resume.
A curriculum vitae (CV) is a detailed document outlining your academic background, research experience, publications, awards, and scholarly achievements.
International Students: Provide Test Scores.
Provide the Office of Graduate Education a score report on the Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) or International English Language Testing System (IELTS). Unofficial documents and Duolingo scores are acceptable in the application system.
The Office of Graduate Education's English proficiency requirements for admission are listed on their website, with particular requirements concerning Graduate Teaching Assistants (GTAs). Explore the
English proficiency requirements
.
Pay the Application Fee.
A non-refundable application fee is required for each application you submit. Visit
the Office of Graduate Education
website for the current fees.
Our program does not provide application fee waivers. If you are a domestic applicant, the Office of Graduate Education may grant an application fee waiver to applicants who meet
specific criteria
.
After Applying: Check Your Application Status.
You may check your application status at any time by signing in to the application portal. You will need the email address and password you used when completing the application.
You will be notified by email to check the application portal when an admission decision has been made.
Graduate applications sometimes remain in "Review" status for many weeks. Our program usually reviews files at scheduled intervals, not daily.
When to Apply
Deadlines
Apply by the deadline for full consideration of available funding opportunities and for first-round review. There is no separate funding application; funding consideration is automatic. All M.S. and Ph.D students are admitted for a Fall Term start date.
Deadline for Ph.D. applications: Dec. 10
Deadline for M.S. applications: Jan. 5
Application Review
When are applications reviewed?
Ph.D. and M.S. applicants are notified of decisions between February and April each year.
Who reviews M.S. and Ph.D. applications and makes admission decisions?
Both the EECS Graduate Admissions Committee and individual faculty review M.S. and Ph.D. applications.
Following the program's recommendation, OSU Graduate Admissions will determine whether the general university admission requirements have been met and will issue the formal admission.
Applicants are evaluated on their entire application, and admission decisions are made based on the strength of each application relative to the applicant pool in any given year. Meeting the minimum application requirements does not guarantee admission.
Ph.D. and M.S. admission decisions are based on many factors, such as the quality of the applicant’s prior academic degree and record of accomplishment, statement of objectives, letters of recommendation, relevant work experience, preparation in the proposed field of study, and the suitability of the applicant’s academic goals with the faculty’s research interests. A critical consideration is whether Oregon State's graduate programs can satisfactorily fulfill your academic objectives.
Application Tips
Start early
Give yourself time to research our program, prepare application materials, and request letters of recommendation.
Apply by the deadline
Applicants must submit their application by the deadline with all necessary materials in order to be considered for admission.
Be strategic
Tailor your statement of purpose to our program and highlight why you think we are a good fit for your interests.
Seek guidance
Reach out to your mentors, your professors, or others who have gone through the process to get advice and feedback on your application materials.

Every student must have a 3.0 GPA in a bachelor's degree program in one of the following fields (or closely related): electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, mathematics, or physics.
Applicants must have or will complete a baccalaureate degree from an accredited college or university before the start of their entry term.

Data Science for the Contemporary Power Grid – ECE 547 (Offered every Fall)
Computer Architecture – ECE 572 (Offered every Winter)

Assistantships
Assistantships
The School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science offers a limited number of graduate teaching assistantships (GTA) and graduate research assistantships (GRA). There is no separate application process. Applicants are automatically considered for available GTA/GRA positions. Funding is prioritized for Ph.D. applicants.
Graduate teaching assistants and research assistants
are appointed on an annual basis. They receive a monthly stipend, 100% tuition remission, 90% student fee remission, and 90% health insurance premium remission.
Funding Offers
At times, funding offers are made at the same time you have received the admission decision. Sometimes, admission decisions and funding offers are staggered, with the admission decision made before the potential funding offer. Whenever there are updates to your application status/funding status, you will be notified to check the application portal.
Accept your funding offer by April 15. This deadline for accepting financial support is set by OSU and many
CGS member institutions
; further admission/funding offers may be issued shortly after that date.
You may be admitted as a graduate student without an offer of funding. This is typically more common with M.S. students. The MEng degree is not eligible for GTA/GRA funding support.
*Award offers and amounts are subject to change at the discretion of department administrators.

Master of Science Requirements
A final oral exam is required for all degrees. The M.S. degree is normally completed in 2-3 years of full-time study or in four years for part-time study. M.S. students intending to continue for a Ph.D. are encouraged to pursue the thesis option.

Master of Engineering Requirements
Master of Engineering Requirements
Strengthen your technical credentials with a Master of Engineering
The Master of Engineering (MEng) is a professional, coursework-only degree designed for individuals seeking practical skills and leadership roles in industry. The program does not require a thesis or research project, allowing students to focus on advanced coursework aligned with their professional goals and job-related skill development.
MEng vs. M.S. Degrees
The Master of Science (M.S.) degree is research-oriented and suited for students interested in advanced technical research or pursuing a Ph.D., typically requiring a thesis or research project. In contrast, the MEng is a course-based degree intended for students who want to apply advanced engineering knowledge in industry without completing a thesis. Read more about the difference between an MEng and an MS degree.
Learn about MEng vs M.S.
MEng Degree Requirements
Oregon State’s MEng program requires 45 credits of advanced coursework, which may include core engineering courses, ethics, technical writing, and elective courses relevant to professional interests. Students typically develop a Program of Study with an advisor during their first term and complete a final portfolio in place of a final oral exam.
View detailed MEng requirements
Paying for your MEng
MEng students are generally self-funded. While all students may apply for graduate research or teaching assistantships, these opportunities are highly competitive and more commonly awarded to Ph.D. and M.S. students. Additional funding options include scholarships, grants, fellowships, loans, and employer tuition reimbursement programs. Learn more about paying for your MEng degree.
Explore MEng payment options
Ph.D. Degree Requirements
Ph.D. Degree Requirements
Ph.D. degrees are typically completed within four years subsequent to the completion of the M.S. degree. Students admitted into the Ph.D. program must pass a qualifying exam and must demonstrate a capability to undertake independent research in order to advance to candidacy as a full Ph.D. student.
